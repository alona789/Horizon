## [Digital vs Physical Games: Ownership Is the Real Issue](https://popcar.bearblog.dev/its-about-ownership/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A blog post argues that the debate between physical and digital games misses the point: the real problem is that consumers do not truly own the digital games they purchase, due to restrictive licenses and DRM. This matters because it highlights a growing consumer rights issue in the gaming industry and beyond, where companies can revoke access to purchased content. It could drive demand for stronger ownership protections and regulation. The post notes that Steam does not apply hard DRM, so users can play offline without the launcher, but many other platforms use restrictive DRM that can render games unplayable if servers shut down. The author calls for legal recognition of digital ownership, including transferability and permanent access.

hackernews · popcar2 · Jul 5, 14:56 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48794750)

**Background**: Digital Rights Management (DRM) refers to access control technologies that limit how digital content can be used, often preventing copying or sharing. Software licensing is a legal framework where consumers purchase a license to use software, not ownership of the software itself. This distinction means companies can revoke access or impose restrictions that would not be possible with physical ownership.

## [Better Models: Worse Tools](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/4/better-models-worse-tools/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Armin Ronacher reports that newer Claude models (Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 5) exhibit worse adherence to tool call schemas than older models, specifically inventing extra fields in Pi's edit tool's nested edits array. This regression is surprising as newer models should improve, and it suggests that training for specific tools (e.g., Claude Code's edit tool) may degrade performance for custom third-party tool schemas, impacting the reliability of coding harnesses like Pi. The issue occurs only in newer Anthropic models (Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 5), where they invent keys not present in the defined schema, causing Pi to reject the tool call and ask for a retry, even though the edit content is typically correct.

rss · Simon Willison · Jul 4, 22:53

**Background**: Tool calling schemas define the expected JSON format for function arguments when LLMs invoke external tools. Models like Claude are trained via reinforcement learning to use specific tools, such as Claude Code's built-in text editor. However, this training can create a bias that causes the model to deviate from other custom schemas, leading to increased errors.

## [Competence Gate: Gating Tool-Use via Internal Confidence Signals](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1unw5un/competence_gate_gating_tooluse_on_a_small_models/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The author released a 10MB LoRA adapter for Qwen3.5-4B that gates tool-use based on the model's internal confidence signals, improving error detection and reducing private query leakage. This approach addresses a key limitation of small language models—poor verbalized confidence—by tapping into internal activations, making them more reliable for tool-use and privacy-sensitive applications. The gate improved error detection with a d′ of 0.46 (95% CI [0.01, 0.89]), and reduced private query sent to public search from 22% to 10% (reduction 0.12, 95% CI [0.02, 0.22]). It also provides traceable answers with citations and confidence bands.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Synthium- · Jul 5, 07:49

**Background**: Small language models often struggle to accurately express their own confidence, tending to overstate it. Recent research shows that internal confidence signals can be extracted from model activations and used to drive behavior. The Competence Gate adapter reads these internal signals to decide when to answer directly or invoke external tools, improving reliability without requiring larger models.

## [USAF: Sparse Fine-Tuning for MoE on Low-Memory GPUs](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1unl62q/if_your_gpu_can_run_inference_it_should_be_able/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new open-source method called USAF enables sparse fine-tuning of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models on GPUs with as little as 12 GB VRAM, demonstrated by fine-tuning Qwen3-30B-A3B on an AMD RX 6750 XT. USAF democratizes fine-tuning of large MoE models by removing the need for high-end GPUs, enabling broader access to model customization for researchers and practitioners. USAF works by training only the sparse expert weights and the router, avoiding full adapter training; it is released under the Apache 2.0 license with no commercial intent.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/tsuyu122 · Jul 4, 21:56

**Background**: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models use multiple specialized sub-networks (experts) and a router to select which experts to activate per input, enabling larger model sizes with lower inference cost. Fine-tuning such models typically requires significant GPU memory due to dense parameter updates. Sparse fine-tuning methods like USAF reduce memory usage by updating only a subset of parameters.
