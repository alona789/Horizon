## [Simon Willison&\#x27;s keynote recaps 2026 LLM developments so far](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/27/2026-in-llms-so-far/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On 25th September 2026, Simon Willison delivered the closing keynote at the WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America in San Jose, and on 27th September he published the annotated slides and notes alongside the YouTube video. The talk traces the past year chronologically, starting with what he calls the November 2025 inflection point — the release of Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.1, which pushed coding agents from &quot;often make mistakes&quot; to &quot;reliable enough to use on a day-to-day basis&quot;. Willison is one of the most closely followed independent practitioners writing about LLMs, so his retrospective serves as a concise, practitioner-level synthesis of a year of rapid releases for developers trying to keep up. The framing of November 2025 as the moment agentic coding crossed from unreliable to dependable is a strong signal about where day-to-day developer workflows have shifted. The keynote&\#x27;s evidence includes Willison&\#x27;s long-running, self-deprecating &quot;generate an SVG of a pelican riding a bicycle&quot; benchmark, which as of November 2025 still produced broken bicycle frames and pelicans that look like ducks for both Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.1. He emphasizes that the two new models were only incremental improvements on their predecessors, and that the jump came from pairing them with their coding agent harnesses — Claude Code, available since February 2025, and the slightly newer Codex.

**Background**: Coding agents are AI systems that do not just autocomplete code but autonomously read files, run commands, edit repositories, and iterate on tasks, usually built on top of a large language model wrapped in a &quot;harness&quot; of tools and prompts. Because model quality improvements are often gradual, the practical usefulness of such agents can appear to jump suddenly when a model crosses a capability threshold on multi-step tasks. Willison&\#x27;s annual-style keynote recaps are a well-known reference point in the AI developer community for tracking which capabilities have actually become usable in practice.
