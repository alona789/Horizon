Asahi Linux has announced official support for Apple&\#x27;s M3 chip, bringing Linux to Apple&\#x27;s latest silicon via open-source drivers. This marks another major step in the project&\#x27;s ongoing effort to enable Linux on all Apple Silicon Macs. Official M3 support is a significant technical milestone that makes Linux a more viable daily-driver option on current-generation Apple hardware. It expands the open-source ecosystem on Apple Silicon and demonstrates the value of community reverse-engineering efforts in the absence of official documentation. Support is achieved through extensive reverse engineering of Apple&\#x27;s SoCs, as Apple provides no public hardware documentation. Community reports still point to remaining gaps such as HDMI output, sleep/wake reliability, and GPU compute performance compared with Apple&\#x27;s Metal backend.

hackernews · mdp2021 · Sep 6, 14:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49586698)

**Background**: Asahi Linux is a volunteer-driven open-source project started by Hector Martin to port the Linux kernel and related software to Apple Silicon-powered Macs. Because Apple does not publish documentation for its custom processors, the project relies heavily on reverse engineering. It is an Arch Linux-based distribution that aims to provide a polished, upstreamed Linux experience on Apple hardware.

## [Isar Aerospace reaches orbit with payload deployment on second flight](https://isaraerospace.com/press/history-for-european-spaceflight-isar-aerospace-reaches-orbit-and-deploys-payloads-on-second-flight) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On 5 September 2026, Isar Aerospace&\#x27;s second Spectrum launch reached orbit from Andøya Spaceport in Norway and successfully deployed its payloads. This marked the first successful orbital flight from Northern Europe and made Andøya the second active orbital spaceport in Europe. This milestone demonstrates that a private European startup can provide sovereign orbital access without relying on the traditional Arianespace/government framework. It strengthens Europe&\#x27;s independent launch capability and gives global customers a new commercial option for sending payloads into low Earth orbit. Spectrum is a 28-meter, two-stage, liquid-fueled rocket designed to lift up to about 1,000 kilograms to low Earth orbit. Isar Aerospace was founded in 2018 as a spin-off from the Technical University of Munich and plans to manufacture roughly 80% of the rocket in-house.

hackernews · mpweiher · Sep 6, 07:21 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49584083)

**Background**: European orbital launch capability has long been centered on government-backed Arianespace, whose launchers typically take off from French Guiana rather than European soil. Isar Aerospace is part of a new wave of European launch startups aiming to provide smaller, cheaper, and more flexible access to orbit. Launching from Andøya, inside the Arctic Circle, is well suited for polar and sun-synchronous orbits, which are useful for Earth-observation satellites.

## [New gTLD Domains: 10–20% Used for Scams, DNS Abuse Crisis](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/6/the-purpose-of-dns-is-to-spread-scams/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A recent Interisle report cited by Terence Eden shows that of 85 million gTLD domain registrations in 2025, 8.5 million were blocklisted by May 2025. Eden and Simon Willison argue this means the Domain Name System has become a primary vector for scams, with an abuse rate likely between 10% and 20%. The statistic implies that one in five newly registered gTLD domains may be tied to scams, making DNS infrastructure a major cybercrime enabler rather than a neutral address book. This puts pressure on ICANN, registries, and registrars to address abuse prevention more aggressively. The numbers come from an Interisle report measuring cybercriminal demand for domains, and Eden calls 10% the &\#x27;likely floor&\#x27; with the real rate probably closer to 20%. Notably, ICANN has reportedly been discussing the problem for years, yet gTLD abuse remains widespread.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 6, 14:40

**Background**: The Domain Name System \(DNS\) translates human-friendly names like example.com into IP addresses. Generic top-level domains \(gTLDs\) such as .com, .org, and newer extensions are one category of top-level domains administered globally. DNS blocklists are curated lists of domains that DNS filters refuse to resolve, so blocklist additions are a common proxy for measuring domain abuse.
