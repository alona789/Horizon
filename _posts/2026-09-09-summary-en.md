## [OpenAI Claims It Has Solved Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wavdi7/openal_says_it_has_cracked_one_of_maths/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI announced via its official website and in a New York Times report that it has produced a solution to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize Problems. The proof has not yet been peer-reviewed or independently verified by the mathematics community. If correct, this would be the first solution of a Millennium Problem since the Poincaré conjecture, and a landmark intersection of AI and pure mathematics. It would also confirm long-suspected mathematical questions about fluid behavior, with wide consequences for physics, engineering, and weather modeling, although experts caution that extraordinary claims require extraordinary scrutiny. The claim concerns the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations, asking whether smooth, globally-defined solutions always exist from smooth initial data. As of 2026, the only officially solved Millennium Prize problem is the Poincaré conjecture, so OpenAI&\#x27;s proposed proof remains unverified by the broader mathematical community.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Shizuka\_Kuze · Sep 8, 17:42

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations are partial differential equations that express momentum conservation for Newtonian fluids, such as water and air. The Millennium Prize Problems, established by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, are seven of the hardest open problems in mathematics, each carrying a one-million-dollar prize. Because the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes system governs turbulent fluid flows, proving that its solutions never blow up or develop singularities in finite time has been a central challenge in both mathematics and physics.

NeurIPS is one of the most prestigious machine-learning conferences, and the Position Paper Track is a venue for well-argued speculative or controversial positions.

A new benchmark of Qwen3.8 27B shows that 4-bit quantized versions preserve most of the model&\#x27;s quality, while 2-bit scores slightly lower and 1-bit collapses completely. It suggests 4-bit quantization is a solid choice for saving VRAM without giving up much capability, while 1-bit remains unusable for real tasks. The chart includes Wilson 95% confidence intervals, though commenters point out these do not measure run-to-run variation; 2-bit scores are also visibly lower than 4-bit. Qwen is a family of open-weight large language models built by Alibaba Cloud. Extreme quantization, such as 1-bit, compresses weights into only a few possible values and can drastically reduce memory use, but it also risks severe degradation in output quality.

High NA EUV is the next advancement in EUV systems, using a higher numerical aperture to achieve finer resolution for the sub-2nm node era. Moving from the industry-standard 6-inch mask to a 12-inch format requires extensive changes throughout the semiconductor manufacturing infrastructure.
