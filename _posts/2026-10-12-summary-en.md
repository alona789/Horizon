## [Terence Tao&\#x27;s &\#x27;Math 2.0&\#x27; Slides Argue AI Must Move Beyond Problem-Solving](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/slides/math-2-0-caltech-2026.pdf) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Fields Medalist Terence Tao published a slide deck titled &quot;Math 2.0&quot; \(dated October 2026, from a Caltech talk\) in which he argues that AI has ended the era of scarce proofs and that mathematics must now decide how to reorganize itself around AI tools. His central claim is that the field should decenter automated solving of open problems and instead focus on sustaining the mathematical community and the role of mathematical understanding. Coming from one of the most prominent living mathematicians, this framing is likely to shape how mathematicians, funders and institutions think about research priorities, grant writing and career paths as AI systems increasingly produce research-level proofs. It also captures a broader anxiety across knowledge professions that AI may hollow out the human purpose of a discipline, not just automate its tedious parts. Tao&\#x27;s talk frames current AI-driven math as optimizing for individual solved problems while ignoring the wider field, offering the metaphor that &quot;open problems are lighthouses, not destinations,&quot; and that today&\#x27;s math AI is not a &quot;good citizen&quot; because it does not try to make elegant additions to the shared body of theory. The deck also contains slides on AI in cancer medicine, which drew sharp criticism from commenters who argued that mechanism-based understanding is not always required for effective treatments.

**Background**: Terence Tao is a Fields Medalist and one of the world&\#x27;s best-known mathematicians, so his public reflections on AI carry unusual weight. Recent advances have seen AI systems, including large language models and specialized provers, produce solutions and proofs for problems that researchers had worked on for years, which is why the phrase &quot;Math 1.0&quot; is used to describe the older era when proofs and solved problems were relatively scarce and human-driven. &quot;Math 2.0&quot; is Tao&\#x27;s proposed next phase, in which AI tools are used to sustain the mathematical community and the human understanding of mathematics rather than only to solve problems.

**Discussion**: The Hacker News thread broadly agreed that AI is genuinely disrupting mathematics, with one commenter reframing Tao&\#x27;s argument in software terms: pure mathematics is a codebase of theory, definitions and proofs, and current AI is not a &quot;good citizen&quot; because it does not make elegant contributions to it. Sentiment was more critical of Tao&\#x27;s medical slides, with commenters working in drug discovery arguing he is out of his lane, noting that many effective medicines \(such as general anesthesia\) work without fully understood mechanisms, and others described researchers grieving over problems AI solved overnight and rewriting grant proposals they had just submitted.
